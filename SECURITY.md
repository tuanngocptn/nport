# Security policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

**Do not open a public issue.**

Use [GitHub's private vulnerability reporting](https://github.com/tuanngocptn/nport/security/advisories/new), or email **tuanngocptn@gmail.com** with `[SECURITY]` in the subject.

Please include: what the issue is, how to reproduce it, what an attacker gains, and the affected version. A proof of concept helps a great deal.

NPort is maintained by one person in their spare time. Expect acknowledgement within a few days and a fix timeline that depends on severity. You will be credited unless you prefer otherwise.

| Version | Status |
| --- | --- |

## In scope

- The control plane at `api.nport.link` — subdomain claiming, lease ownership, provisioning
- The CLI and desktop app, including credential handling and the connector implementation
- The website at `nport.link`
- Anything letting one user affect another's tunnel, or claim a name they should not have

Particularly interested in: bypassing lease ownership to delete or hijack someone else's subdomain; claiming a reserved name; causing NPort to delete a DNS record it does not own; bypassing the server-authoritative time limit; extracting a tunnel token from a client artifact or a log; or defeating the abuse controls cheaply.

## Out of scope

- **Creating tunnels without an account.** That is the product (see [ADR-0007](docs/DECISIONS.md)). Rate-limit *bypass* is in scope; the absence of authentication is not.
- **Content served through someone's tunnel.** NPort does not proxy or inspect tunnel traffic — it goes from Cloudflare's edge straight to the user's machine. For abuse of a specific `*.nport.link` URL, see below.
- Vulnerabilities in Cloudflare's platform — report those to [Cloudflare](https://hackerone.com/cloudflare).
- Missing security headers on the marketing site with no demonstrated impact.
- Reports generated by a scanner with no verified exploit path.

## Threat model

NPort is **account-free by design**, so the control plane cannot authenticate *who* is calling. It can only make claims verifiable and abuse expensive. The relevant properties:

- **Ownership without identity.** Creating a tunnel returns a 256-bit `ownerToken` once; only its SHA-256 is stored. Modifying or deleting a lease requires it.
- **No unprovable deletes.** NPort will not delete a DNS record unless it is a CNAME pointing at exactly the tunnel ID it expects.
- **Server-authoritative expiry.** Time limits are enforced by the server, never by the client.
- **No secrets in client artifacts.** Nothing shipped to a user contains a key or token.
- **No tunnel traffic on NPort infrastructure.** There is no request log to leak, by construction.

Full detail in [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) §7.

## Reporting abuse of a tunnel

To report a `*.nport.link` URL used for phishing, malware, or other abuse, email **tuanngocptn@gmail.com** with `[ABUSE]` in the subject and the full URL. Tunnels are short-lived and most will have expired before a report arrives, but persistent abuse gets the subdomain blocked. Triage is in [docs/OPERATIONS.md](docs/OPERATIONS.md).
